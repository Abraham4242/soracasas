Moving to Turks & Caicos in 2026

Grace Bay, the US dollar, 75 minutes from Miami, and 0% on every direct tax — but one of the most expensive and demanding places to relocate in the Caribbean. Here's the real picture.

By SORA Real Estate Editorial · 9 min read · Updated July 2026

Turks & Caicos sells one of the Caribbean's most seductive packages: Grace Bay (routinely voted the world's best beach), the US dollar, English, a 75-minute hop from Miami, and zero direct tax — no income, capital-gains, estate, or property tax. The catch is equally clear: it's one of the most expensive places to live in the region, and the residency system sets a high bar. This is a destination for people with real means.

Taxes: zero direct tax

TCI applies a 0% rate on all direct taxes — no personal income tax, no corporate tax, no capital-gains tax, no estate/gift tax, and no annual property tax or VAT. The government instead raises revenue through import duty, stamp duty on property, and a tax on rental income. (US citizens still file with the IRS.)

Residency & permits

To live legally you need one of three documents from the Department of Immigration:

  • Temporary Residence Certificate (independent means/investment): a qualifying investment of $500,000 on Providenciales/West Caicos, or $250,000 on the other islands; renewed annually, no local work.
  • Work Permit: employer-sponsored, demanding (labor-market tests, medicals, delays).
  • Permanent Residence Certificate (PRC): after ~10 years of residence, or via major investment (approaching/above $1M) — no stay requirements once held.

Citizenship (British Overseas Territories) becomes possible after 5 years (including 12 months holding a PRC).

Cost of living — high

This is the main trade-off. High rents (fierce competition from tourist rentals), plus energy, food, and transport well above North American prices, make TCI one of the priciest Caribbean bases. The zero-tax framework is the offset. Test-drive life on a tourist stay before committing.

Is Turks & Caicos safe?

Level 1 · Normal precautions — a British Overseas Territory with generally low crime and a large expat community on Providenciales; use ordinary precautions.

Generally yes — a stable British Overseas Territory with a large, established expat community and ordinary big-town precautions. As anywhere, secure your home and stay aware in nightlife areas.

Where expats actually live

The roughly 25,000 expats are overwhelmingly on Providenciales ('Provo'), home to Grace Bay and most services; the administrative capital, Cockburn Town, sits on the quieter Grand Turk.

Is Turks & Caicos right for you?

  • Great fit if: you want a US-dollar, English, zero-tax base minutes from Miami and can fund a $250K–$500K+ investment or a strong job offer.
  • Think twice if: you're cost-sensitive, or you want an easy, low-bar residency route — TCI is neither cheap nor simple.

Frequently asked questions

Does Turks & Caicos have income tax?

No. Turks & Caicos applies 0% on all direct taxes — no personal income tax, corporate tax, capital-gains tax, estate/gift tax, VAT, or annual property tax. Revenue comes from import duty, property stamp duty, and a tax on rental income. US citizens still file with the IRS.

How do you get residency in Turks & Caicos?

Through a Temporary Residence Certificate (investment of $500,000 on Providenciales/West Caicos or $250,000 on other islands), an employer-sponsored work permit, or a Permanent Residence Certificate (after about 10 years, or via major investment near/above $1M). Citizenship is possible after 5 years including 12 months on a PRC.

Is Turks & Caicos expensive to live in?

Yes — it's one of the most expensive places to live in the Caribbean, driven by high rents and imported goods, energy, and food well above North American prices. The zero direct-tax regime is the main offset.

Where do expats live in Turks & Caicos?

The great majority of the roughly 25,000 expats live on Providenciales ('Provo'), home to Grace Bay Beach and most services and jobs. The administrative capital, Cockburn Town, is on the quieter Grand Turk.
