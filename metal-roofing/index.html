Metal Roofing

Standing-seam and metal panel roofs — the tropical champion: reflects heat, sheds rain instantly, shrugs off hurricanes, and lasts 40–70 years. What it costs and why it wins.

By SORA Real Estate Editorial · 7 min read · Updated July 2026

Metal roofing is the closest thing to a tropical all-rounder there is. A quality standing-seam roof reflects solar heat instead of soaking it up, sheds torrential rain in seconds, carries the best hurricane-wind ratings of any common roof, and lasts 40–70 years — often the last roof a house ever needs. Its secret is the concealed fastener: no exposed screw holes to leak or work loose in the wind.

How it's built

Metal roofing comes in two families. Standing seam uses interlocking vertical panels whose fasteners are hidden inside the raised seams — nothing penetrates the weather surface, which is why it's the premium, leak- and wind-resistant choice. Exposed-fastener panels (screwed through the face, the corrugated/ribbed sheets covered on our tin roof page) are cheaper but rely on gaskets that age. Panels are typically steel (galvanized/Galvalume), aluminum (best for coastal salt), or occasionally zinc/copper, laid over a solid deck with an underlayment and, on a home, insulation.

What does it cost?

Roughly $$–$$$: standing seam runs about $80–160/m² ($8–15/sq ft) installed, exposed-fastener metal notably less. That's more upfront than shingles, but the 40–70-year lifespan means it's usually cheaper per year of service — you're buying one roof instead of three. Near the coast, budget for aluminum or premium coatings to beat salt corrosion. (Ranges are approximate and vary by metal, profile, and region.)

Buildability — how hard is it to actually build?

Metal roofing is a professional install, not a weekend DIY — standing seam especially needs specialist crews and roll-forming, and the whole performance case rests on correct edge detailing and anchoring (the perimeter is where wind gets under a roof). The good news in the tropics: skilled metal roofers are common across Panama and Costa Rica, materials are readily available, and it permits and finances without any friction. Get a crew that knows coastal detailing and hurricane fastening and you have a roof that outlasts almost everything under it.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a metal roof last?

A quality standing-seam metal roof lasts 40–70 years, and premium metals like zinc or copper even longer. Exposed-fastener panel roofs last less (the gaskets around screws age first), but still typically outlast asphalt shingles by decades.

Is a metal roof good in a hurricane?

Yes — properly installed standing-seam metal carries some of the best wind ratings of any residential roof because there are no exposed fasteners to work loose and the panels lock together. The critical detail is anchoring the edges and following the manufacturer's high-wind installation spec.

Does a metal roof make the house hotter?

The opposite — metal reflects a large share of solar radiation rather than absorbing it like a dark shingle, so with a reflective finish and proper insulation a metal roof keeps interiors cooler and cuts cooling bills, a real advantage in the tropics.
