Tile Roof vs Asphalt Shingles

A century-lasting inert roof versus the cheap familiar default — the widest gap in lifespan of any roofing matchup. When the extra cost of tile pays off, and when shingles make sense.

By SORA Real Estate Editorial · 6 min read · Updated July 2026

This matchup has the widest lifespan gap in roofing: a tile roof that can last a century against asphalt shingles that last 12–25 years. Tile costs more upfront and needs a stronger structure; shingles are cheap and quick. In a harsh tropical climate the case for tile — or for skipping shingles entirely — gets stronger.

The quick verdict

For a permanent tropical home, tile wins on every axis except upfront cost and weight — it lasts 3–5× longer, runs cooler, and resists humidity. Shingles make sense only for the tightest budgets or short-term ownership; if you go that route, use architectural, algae-resistant, high-wind versions.

How they actually differ

Shingles buy time and cash flow; tile buys permanence. The shingle's appeal is entirely front-loaded — cheap and fast — while every other column favors tile, which simply outlasts and outperforms it. The one real cost of tile beyond money is weight: the structure has to be built for it. If it is, tile is the roof you never replace; shingles are the roof you replace on schedule.

Which should you choose?

  • Choose tile for a home you'll keep, a cooler house, a colonial look, and a roof that outlives the mortgage — when the structure is designed for the load.
  • Choose shingles only when upfront cost is the hard limit or the hold is short — and upgrade to architectural, high-wind, algae-resistant shingles if you do.

…in the tropics specifically

The tropics punish shingles — UV shortens their life, humidity streaks them with algae, and storms lift them — while everything tile does well (staying cool, ignoring humidity, lasting for generations) matters more here. For most permanent Panama or Costa Rica homes, tile (or metal) is the better long-run investment, and shingles are the compromise you make only for budget.

Frequently asked questions

Is a tile roof worth it over shingles?

For a home you'll keep in a hot or humid climate, usually yes — tile lasts 50–100 years versus 12–25 for shingles, runs cooler, and resists algae, so despite the higher upfront cost and heavier structure it's cheaper per year and far more durable.

Why are tile roofs more expensive than shingles?

Tile costs more to buy and to install, and its weight requires a stronger, engineered roof structure. That upfront premium buys a roof that lasts several times longer and performs better in heat and humidity, so the lifetime cost often favors tile.

Can I put a tile roof on a house built for shingles?

Not without reinforcement. Tile is much heavier than shingles, so a structure designed for a light shingle roof usually needs strengthening to carry tile. That's why the choice is best made early in the design.
