Tin & Corrugated Metal Roofs

The affordable workhorse of Latin American roofs. Cheap, quick, and it sheds rain beautifully. Done right it's a genuinely good tropical roof; done cheap it's hot and rusts. The straight version.

By SORA Real Estate Editorial · 6 min read · Updated July 2026

"Tin roof" really means corrugated or ribbed metal sheet, usually galvanized steel or zinc-aluminum, screwed to purlins in wavy or trapezoidal profiles. It's the most common roof in Latin America for three good reasons. It's cheap, it goes up fast, and it sheds tropical rain without complaint. The catch is that a bare metal sheet with nothing under it is an oven and a drum, and a thin coating rusts within sight of the sea. Spec it well and it's a smart tropical roof. Spec it cheap and you'll feel every degree and hear every raindrop.

What's under a corrugated roof

Sheets get fastened with exposed screws, each fitted with a neoprene washer, to timber or steel purlins, and overlapped at the sides and ends to move water off. Two upgrades separate a good corrugated roof from a bad one. The first is insulation, a foil-and-foam layer or an insulated sandwich panel that stops heat and condensation. The second is a quality coating, Galvalume or a coastal-grade paint system that fights rust. After that, the fastening pattern and the overlaps decide how it behaves in wind.

What it costs

This is the $ option. A basic corrugated roof runs roughly $25 to $60 per square metre installed, or $2.50 to $5.50 a square foot, a fraction of standing seam or tile. Insulated panel systems cost more but are still affordable and transform how the house feels. That low price and speed are exactly why it's everywhere. Ranges depend on the profile, the gauge, and the coating.

Getting it right on site

Corrugated metal is one of the simplest roofs to install, which is half its appeal. Small crews put it up fast and the materials are everywhere in Panama and Costa Rica. The skill isn't in laying the sheets, it's in the three things cheap jobs skip. Insulate, or the house bakes. Match the coating to how close you are to salt water, or it rusts. And use a wind-rated fastening pattern with proper overlaps, or a storm finds the edges. Cover those and a corrugated roof is a sharp, affordable choice. Ignore them and it becomes the roof people complain about.

Frequently asked questions

Is a tin or corrugated roof good for a hot climate?

It can be, but only with insulation. Bare corrugated metal radiates the heat it absorbs straight into the house. Add a foil-and-foam layer or use insulated sandwich panels and it performs well; skip insulation to save money and the interior gets uncomfortably hot.

How long does a corrugated metal roof last?

Usually 15 to 40 years, depending heavily on the coating and how close you are to salt air. A basic galvanized sheet near the coast rusts sooner; a Galvalume or aluminum sheet with a good paint system inland lasts a lot longer.

Is a tin roof noisy when it rains?

A bare corrugated roof over open purlins is loud, the classic drumming. Installed over a deck with insulation, the noise drops sharply. The sound is a construction choice, not something inherent to metal.
