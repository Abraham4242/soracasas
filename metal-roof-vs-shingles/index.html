Metal Roof vs Shingles

The two roofs most people choose between. One costs more upfront and lasts three times as long. A builder's side-by-side on cost, lifespan, and which wins in a hot, stormy climate.

By SORA Real Estate Editorial · 6 min read · Updated July 2026

This is the roofing decision most homeowners actually face. On one side, the familiar, cheap asphalt shingle. On the other, the pricier, far longer-lived metal roof. Shingles win on upfront price and nothing else. Metal wins on lifespan, durability, heat, and storms. Which one is right for you depends on how long you'll own the house and how rough your climate is.

The short answer

For a permanent home in a hot, wet, hurricane-prone place, metal wins clearly. It costs more today but lasts three times as long, reflects heat, and rides out storms. Pick shingles only if lowest upfront cost is the deciding factor or you're holding the property short term.

What actually separates them

It comes down to time horizon. Shingles are cheaper the day they go on and more expensive every year after, because you re-roof two or three times in the span one metal roof lasts. Add the tropics, with brutal UV, constant humidity, and hurricanes, and shingles age faster while metal's strengths matter more.

Which one is right for you

  • Go metal if it's your permanent home, you're in a hot or hurricane-prone place, or you want to stop thinking about the roof.
  • Go shingles if upfront budget is the hard limit, the hold is short, or you're matching a neighborhood look.
  • There isn't much of a middle, but high-wind architectural shingles are the best shingle option if you take that route.

In a tropical climate

In Panama or Costa Rica the case for metal is even stronger than up north. Equatorial UV cooks shingles toward the low end of their short life, humidity feeds algae, and storm wind goes for the shingle's sealant bond. A properly anchored standing-seam metal roof sidesteps all three, which is why it's our default.

Frequently asked questions

Is a metal roof worth the extra cost over shingles?

Usually yes for a home you'll keep. Metal costs more upfront but lasts 40 to 70 years against 12 to 25 for shingles, so you buy one roof instead of two or three. Over the life of the house it's typically cheaper per year and performs far better in heat and storms.

Which is better in a hurricane, metal or shingles?

Metal, clearly. A properly anchored standing-seam roof carries some of the best wind ratings available, while asphalt shingles are the common roof most likely to lift and tear off. The fastening and edge detailing matter as much as the material.

Are metal roofs noisier than shingles?

A little, but far less than people expect. Over a solid deck with insulation, the normal way metal goes on a home, rain noise is modest. The loud drumming comes from bare metal over open framing, not a properly built roof.
