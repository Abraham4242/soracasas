Moving to Dominica in 2026

The 'Nature Island' — the cheapest cost of living in the Caribbean, one of the world's most affordable second passports ($200K), Level-1 safety, and rainforest instead of resorts. Here's the honest picture.

By SORA Real Estate Editorial · 9 min read · Updated July 2026

Dominica (not to be confused with the Dominican Republic) is the Caribbean's 'Nature Island' — all rainforest, waterfalls, and hot springs, with almost no big resorts. It's also the region's cheapest place to live, home to one of the world's most affordable second passports (from $200,000), and rated Level 1 for safety. If a tranquil, eco-oriented life appeals more than nightlife and beach clubs, few islands offer more for less.

Citizenship by investment (from $200K)

Dominica runs one of the oldest (est. 1993) and cheapest CBI programs, fully remote with no residency requirement:

  • Economic Diversification Fund donation: from $200,000 single ($250,000 for a family of four).
  • Real estate: from $200,000 in an approved project, held 3 years (5 if the next buyer is also a CBI applicant).
  • Timeline: ~6–9 months; the passport gives visa-free/visa-on-arrival access to ~145 countries; no wealth, foreign-income, or capital-gains tax for non-resident citizens.

Cost of living — the cheapest in the Caribbean

This is Dominica's superpower. A single person lives comfortably on $1,500–$2,000/month; a centrally-located one-bedroom rents from ~$280, and a three-bedroom house can be bought for under $100,000. Imported electronics and specialty foods cost more, but everyday local life is genuinely inexpensive (full-time domestic help runs $400–$600/month).

Residency (beyond CBI)

US citizens can stay up to 6 months without a visa. There's a temporary residency option for those with independent means who don't want to work or buy citizenship, plus a working visa tied to a job offer of 6+ months.

Is Dominica safe?

Level 1 · Normal precautions — Dominica is one of the safer islands in the region — violent crime is rare and communities are close-knit; the main concerns are petty theft and natural disasters.

Yes — the US State Department rates it Level 1, and violent crime is rare (fewer incidents than Jamaica or Trinidad). Portsmouth is often cited as the safest town. Plan instead for the real risks: occasional power outages, rural roads, and hurricane exposure.

Healthcare

Functional but basic — the Dominica–China Friendship Hospital in Roseau covers general and emergency care, but the island lacks advanced specialists and equipment, so serious cases are evacuated to nearby islands or the US. Comprehensive international insurance with medical-evacuation cover is essential.

Where expats actually live

The main expat spots are Roseau (the capital, strongest rental market), Portsmouth (safe, close-knit), and coastal Marigot and Soufrière. About 20% of the island is protected parkland, and a new international airport (targeted for 2027) will finally bring direct flights from North America and Europe.

Is Dominica right for you?

  • Great fit if: you want the lowest costs in the Caribbean, a cheap fast passport, real safety, and an eco/nature lifestyle over resorts.
  • Think twice if: you need advanced local healthcare, resort-style beaches and nightlife, or easy air access today (that improves in 2027).

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to live in Dominica?

Dominica is the cheapest place to live in the Caribbean. A single person lives comfortably on $1,500–$2,000/month; a central one-bedroom rents from about $280, and a three-bedroom house can be bought for under $100,000. Imported goods cost more, but everyday local life is genuinely inexpensive.

How much is Dominica citizenship by investment?

From $200,000 via a donation to the Economic Diversification Fund ($250,000 for a family of four), or from $200,000 in approved real estate held 3–5 years. The process is fully remote with no residency requirement, takes about 6–9 months, and the passport reaches ~145 countries visa-free.

Is Dominica safe?

Yes — the US State Department rates it Level 1 (Exercise Normal Precautions). Violent crime is rare and communities are close-knit (Portsmouth is often cited as safest). The main concerns are petty theft, occasional power outages, rural roads, and hurricane exposure.

Is Dominica the same as the Dominican Republic?

No — they're different countries. Dominica is a small, English-speaking, mountainous 'Nature Island' in the eastern Caribbean; the Dominican Republic is a much larger Spanish-speaking country on the island of Hispaniola. This guide covers Dominica; see our separate Dominican Republic guide.
