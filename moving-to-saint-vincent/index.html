Moving to St Vincent & the Grenadines in 2026

Thirty-two islands, an unspoiled yachting paradise (Bequia, Mustique), an accessible residency path, and — new for remote workers — fiber internet across the Grenadines. Here's the honest picture.

By SORA Real Estate Editorial · 9 min read · Updated July 2026

St Vincent & the Grenadines is 32 islands (9 inhabited) of genuinely unspoiled Caribbean — a sailing and snorkeling paradise that runs from ordinary, affordable Vincentian life on the main island to the ultra-exclusive private islands of Mustique and Canouan. It's relatively affordable, relatively safe, English-speaking, and now — thanks to new fiber across the Grenadines — genuinely workable for remote workers. There's no golden visa, but the residency path is refreshingly accessible.

Cost of living

Affordable by Caribbean standards (if you avoid the luxury islands). A single person spends around $1,241/month including modest rent ($957 excluding it); a one-bedroom in Kingstown runs ~$407. The EC dollar is pegged at 2.70 to US$1. Note living on Bequia, Mustique, or Canouan is pricier than the main island.

Residency — accessible, no golden visa

Refreshingly straightforward: a temporary residence permit (typically one year) is available to those with a work permit, a business, or independent means with proof of sufficient funds — the financial bar isn't high — or via property ownership. Permanent residency comes after seven years, with a clear path to citizenship. Most Western nationals enter visa-free for 1–6 months. Buying land requires an Alien Land Holding License (duties up to ~17%), though property taxes afterward are very low.

Is St Vincent safe?

Level 1 · Normal precautions — relatively safe and measurably calmer than many regional peers — petty theft spikes around Kingstown, but violent crime rarely targets visitors; the active La Soufrière volcano is a natural risk to monitor.

Relatively — most visitors and residents have no issues, and it's measurably calmer than several regional peers. Petty theft rises around Kingstown; violent crime rarely targets visitors. The real thing to monitor is nature: La Soufrière is an active volcano (it erupted in 2021), and hurricane season disrupts flights and ferries.

Healthcare

This needs the most caution. Primary care exists across ~39 facilities, with the Milton Cato Memorial Hospital (~200 beds) in Kingstown for secondary care — but serious cases require evacuation to Barbados, Trinidad, or the US, so air-evacuation insurance is essential.

Remote work: now viable

A genuine recent upgrade: a submarine fiber cable now links the main island to Bequia, Mustique, Canouan, and Union Island, with Flow and Digicel rolling out fiber-to-the-home — practical speeds multiplied many times over, suitable for video calls and cloud work.

Where expats actually live

  • Main island (best value + services): the southwest strip from Villa to Calliaqua via Arnos Vale — beaches, shops, near Kingstown.
  • Bequia: the balanced Grenadines pick — real island life, decent services, an established expat community.
  • Mustique & Canouan: ultra-luxury private-island territory (properties often $1M+).

Is St Vincent right for you?

  • Great fit if: you want unspoiled, affordable, sailing-centric Caribbean life with an easy residency path — and now the connectivity to work remotely.
  • Think twice if: you need advanced local healthcare, or you want turnkey services and infrastructure (the property market is informal and services are limited).
