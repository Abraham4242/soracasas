Asphalt Shingles

North America's default roof — cheap, familiar, fast. In the tropics they're usually the wrong call: UV and heat cook them, humidity grows algae, and hurricanes lift them. The honest breakdown.

By SORA Real Estate Editorial · 6 min read · Updated July 2026

Asphalt shingles cover roughly 80% of North American homes — a fiberglass mat coated in asphalt and mineral granules, nailed down in overlapping rows. They're cheap, familiar, come in every color, and any roofer can install them fast. That's the whole case for them. In a hot, humid, hurricane-prone climate, though, the very things that make shingles convenient up north work against them — which is why you see far fewer of them on well-built tropical homes.

How they're built

Shingles are nailed in overlapping courses over an underlayment on a solid deck, starting from the eaves up. 3-tab shingles are the flat, cheap version; architectural (dimensional) shingles are thicker, longer-lasting, and better-looking. Sealant strips bond each course to the one below so wind can't get under them — a bond that UV and heat slowly break down.

What does it cost?

Shingles are the $ roof: about $30–70/m² ($3–6.50/sq ft) installed, the cheapest common option upfront. But with a tropical lifespan often at the low end of the 12–25-year range, the cost-per-year can quietly exceed a metal or tile roof you'd only buy once. Cheap today, not always cheap over the life of the house. (Approximate; 3-tab vs architectural drives the range.)

Buildability — how hard is it to actually build?

Ease of installation is asphalt's real strength: it's the simplest, fastest, most widely-known roof to lay, which is exactly why it dominates volume homebuilding. The buildability question in the tropics isn't can you install it — anyone can — it's should you. Under intense equatorial UV the asphalt binder dries and cracks faster, sustained humidity feeds algae, and the wind-sealant bond that keeps shingles down is the first thing a hurricane exploits. If you use shingles here, choose architectural, algae-resistant, high-wind-rated versions and know you're trading long-term performance for a lower upfront price. For most permanent tropical homes, metal or tile is the better investment.

Frequently asked questions

How long do asphalt shingles last in a tropical climate?

Asphalt shingles last 12–25 years in temperate climates, but tropical conditions push that toward the low end — intense UV degrades the asphalt binder faster and constant humidity encourages algae. Architectural (dimensional) shingles last longer than cheap 3-tab, but still fall well short of metal or tile.

Are asphalt shingles good in hurricanes?

They're the weakest common roof in high wind. Shingles rely on a sealant bond and nails to stay down, and hurricane gusts routinely lift and tear them off. If shingles are used in a hurricane zone, high-wind-rated products and a proper nailing pattern are essential — but metal and concrete perform far better.

Why are asphalt shingles so common if they don't last as long?

Because they're the cheapest roof to buy and the fastest to install, which suits volume homebuilding in temperate North America. In the tropics, where UV, humidity, and hurricanes are harsher, that upfront saving is often outweighed by the shorter lifespan and lower storm resistance.
